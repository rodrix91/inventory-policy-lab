# 0015: Cost-optimised safety stock and order quantity

- Status: accepted (alpha), opt-in
- Date: 2026-10-16
- Deciders: Rodrigo Pantoja Navajas

## Context

With ADRs 0012 and 0014 every replay of the test window has a price, but the policies themselves are still set by a service target (the safety stock) and by EOQ or a cover rule (the order quantity); the cost only ranks them afterwards. The obvious next question is whether choosing `s` and `S` (or `s` and `Q`) for cost directly does better out of sample than the heuristic, and how it compares with simply asking (R, S) for a higher service level (the open question of ADR 0013). Any such choice must be made on the training window only, or the out-of-sample comparison means nothing.

## Decision

- **Opt-in**: `--optimize-cost` (`Config.optimize_cost`), with cost rates (ADR 0012/0014, so with `--test-periods`) and `--policy sQ` or `RsS`. (R, S) is refused (exit 2): its only parameter is `S`, which a higher `--service-level` already moves, and that is what the README compares it with.
- **What is searched: the safety stock `SS` and the quantity `Q`.** The level of each test period keeps the forecast part of the normal heuristic level, `D x (L + R)` (static, or period by period with `--dynamic-levels`), and gets the searched `SS`; `(R, s, S)` orders up to `S = s + Q`. So the pair `(SS, Q)` is the pair `(s, S)` or `(s, Q)`, and the search also works with dynamic levels without a separate level per period.
- **Objective: expected cost of the test window on demand paths built from training only.** Each of `--optimize-samples` paths (100 by default, at least 10) gives every test period the training fit's forecast of that period plus one of the training one-step rolling-origin forecast errors (the errors of ADR 0008 over one period), drawn with replacement, floored at zero. With fewer than two errors, the training demand's deviations from its mean are drawn instead (noted as `demand deviations`). Each path is replayed from the stock and orders the normal policy left at the end of training, which is where the real test starts, and priced with the SKU's rates; the mean over paths is the expected cost. All candidates are scored on the same paths (common random numbers), drawn from their own seeded generator (`seed:sku:optimize`), so the search is reproducible and does not change the bootstrap draws of the other replays.
- **Why paths and not the training replay itself.** Scoring a candidate by replaying it on the training periods would choose the levels that best fit 12 or 36 known periods, starting from a stock that depends on the candidate; with 12 training months that is fitting noise. Paths built from the forecast and its errors estimate the cost of the *test* window, under the training model, without looking at it. Their limit is that they trust that model: they carry the one-step error but not the error of the forecast's own level, trend and season, so they understate the risk of the frozen forecast (see Consequences).
- **Bounded grid, then a local refinement.** `SS` takes the heuristic value, `z x sigma` for `z` = -1, -0.5, ..., 3 (`sigma`: population standard deviation of the one-step errors times `sqrt(L + R)`), and minus 10%, 20%, ..., 100% of the forecast over `L + R` (down to a level of zero; an (s, Q) level counts the review period in `L + R`, one period more than its replay's risk interval, so its cheapest level can sit well below the heuristic one). `Q` takes the heuristic value and 0.5 to 6 (by 0.5), 8, 10 and 12 periods of mean training demand. Every pair is scored (at most 20 x 16 = 320), then the best `SS` is moved by -4 to +4 quarter-sigma steps at the best `Q` (8 more). The heuristic pair is scored first and kept on ties, so the search moves only for a strictly lower expected cost. With no training demand only the heuristic `Q` is left.
- **Evaluation**: the picked pair is replayed on the real test (fixed origin), with the normal policy on the training periods, and priced like the other replays. The rolling origin is not re-optimised (the report says so); a search at every origin would multiply the cost by the number of origins.
- **Reporting, only with the option.** Markdown: a "Cost-optimised levels" section after the cost section, with the grid and objective in words, a table of normal, bootstrap and cost-optimised replays (fill rate, units short, average stock, orders, total cost of the test) and one row per SKU (safety stock and quantity, expected cost on the training paths and test cost, normal against optimised, and fill rates). JSON: `optimize_cost` and `optimize_samples` in the settings; `out_of_sample.optimized` with `origin`, `samples`, `grid`, `summary` (totals, cost, expected costs) and `skus` (picked and heuristic values, expected costs, candidates, error source and count, test levels, replay and cost). API: `OptimizedReplay`, `SkuOutOfSample.optimized`, `OutOfSampleReport.optimized_cost()`, `optimized_sku_cost(sku)`, `optimized_totals()`, and the module `inventory_policy_lab.optimize` (`search_levels`, `expected_costs`, `demand_paths`, `safety_grid`, `quantity_grid`, `CostSearch`). Without the option the reports are byte for byte those of 0.8.0 (104 outputs compared).
- **No leak, tested**: setting every test period of the 48-month series to zero, or to ten times its demand, leaves every SKU's search (picked pair, expected costs, candidate count) and test levels identical, while the optimised replay's score changes. The path cost is also checked against the simulator: pricing a path after the training warm-up equals pricing `simulate` / `simulate_rss` over training plus path (90 random cases, (s, Q) and (R, s, S) with R = 1 and 2).
- **Time** (best of two, wall clock, one process per run, this machine): the search adds about 1.5 s to the 48-month series (3 SKUs: 0.15 s without, 1.7 s with) and 5.5 s to the 24-month example (11 searched SKUs: 0.12 s to 5.8 s), about 0.5 s per SKU at 100 paths; 200 paths double it (3.2 s and 11.3 s).

## Consequences

- On the README runs (`--test-periods 12 --trend-seasonal`, 50 per order, 1 per unit-month, 10 per unit short) the search cuts the expected cost on the training paths by 33% to 41% in every case, but the test cost follows only twice: 24-month (s, Q) at 18,016 against 21,371 (normal) and 19,212 (bootstrap), and 48-month (R, s, S) at 5,119 against 6,133 (normal) but not the bootstrap's 4,945. On 48-month (s, Q) (7,766 against 5,474) and 24-month (R, s, S) (19,102 against 15,576) it is clearly worse. The fill rate always falls (to 86% to 95%): the paths trust the training forecast and pick too little stock. That is overfitting to the training model rather than to the test, and the results hold with 200 paths or another seed (within about 3%).
- (R, S) R = 1 with a higher service level is the stronger baseline on the 48-month series: at 0.98 it costs 4,474 at 98.5% fill rate, the cheapest fixed-origin static policy there. On the 24-month example raising its level from 0.95 to 0.99 lifts the fill rate only from 90.9% to 93.9% and the cost from 17,313 to 17,758: the frozen forecasts, not the safety factor, cause its shortages.
- So the option is a diagnostic, not a recommendation: it shows how much of the heuristic's cost the training model thinks is avoidable, and the out-of-sample column shows how much really is. Possible improvements, not done here: paths that refit the forecast on bootstrapped histories (model uncertainty), searching at every rolling origin, and a penalty or floor on the fill rate.
